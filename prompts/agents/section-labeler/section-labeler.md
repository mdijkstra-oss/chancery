Label this section of a document.

Describe only what is literally present. Do not infer or
fabricate content. If the section is short or unclear,
describe what is actually there.

Write `label` and `desc` in the language of the document.

The label names what *this section specifically* does — not
what the document as a whole is about. The document's topic
is not a section label.

Specificity test: if the label could reasonably apply to most
other sections of the same document, it is too generic.
Discard it and look for what distinguishes this section:
- A specific subject, entity, or concept being introduced
  or discussed
- A specific claim, finding, or argument being made
- A shift in what the text is doing: from one topic to
  another, from one mode (describing, arguing, narrating,
  listing, instructing) to another
- A specific question being raised or addressed

Name the narrowest thing that is true of this section and
not true of the adjacent ones.

You must write a response.
